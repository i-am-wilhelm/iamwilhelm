<section class="part" aria-label="I">
  <p class="part-num">I.</p>
  <p class="part-lede">The stars were letters first.</p>
  <p class="part-aside" lang="grc">Πλειάδες: seven, and the eye finds six.</p>

The Greek says so in one word. <span lang="grc">Στοιχεῖον</span>: a letter of the alphabet, a physical element, and, in the later grammar, a heavenly body. One wordform holding the alphabet, the material world, and the sky. Flatten the word and you lose the sentence it was keeping: matter is written, and the sky is the oldest text we still read.

It was read for survival before it was read for meaning. Hesiod hands the farmer the sky as an instruction manual: when the Pleiades rise, daughters of Atlas, begin the harvest; when they set, the plough (<em>Works and Days</em>, 383). Seven sisters, and the eye finds six. Every alphabet keeps a letter the eye loses.

</section>

<section class="part" aria-label="II">
  <p class="part-num">II.</p>

Pythagoras heard the text. He wrote nothing; the doctrine reaches us through Aristotle, and Aristotle preserves the best part: the Pythagoreans held that the spinning bodies sound a harmony we fail to notice because it has played since the moment of our birth (<em>De Caelo</em> II.9). A sound present from every birth, for every generation, for the whole life of the species, stops registering as sound. It becomes structure. That is what I mean when I say the music of the spinning bodies speaks an evolutionary language to the soul: we were steeped in the signal before we had ears, and the signal did some of the writing.

Plato scored it and spelled it. In the <em>Republic</em>, Er watches the cosmos turn with a Siren on each whorl, each singing one note, eight notes concording into a single harmony (X, 617b). In the <em>Timaeus</em> he goes further down the alphabet than anyone: fire, air, water, and earth are commonly called the <em>stoicheia</em>, the letters, and Plato objects that they are not even syllables. The true letters are the triangles beneath them, assembling into the five perfect solids, the characters matter is written in. The elements are words. The geometry is the alphabet.

</section>

<section class="part" aria-label="III">
  <p class="part-num">III.</p>

Kepler read Plato literally and nested those five solids between the six planetary orbits (<em>Mysterium Cosmographicum</em>, 1596). The model was wrong. He kept listening for twenty more years, and the harmony finally gave up a true law, the one that still holds every satellite over your head tonight (<em>Harmonices Mundi</em>, 1619). He heard the heavens as "a continuous song for several voices, perceived by the intellect," and he knew exactly whose mail he was opening: "God himself has waited six thousand years for a witness."

That sentence carries the other thing I believe about ideas. Every idea already exists; the commons hold all of them, and an idea only borrows a person. It presents itself to someone, and if that someone sits on it, it withdraws and presents itself through another. The harmony of the spheres waited out Pythagoras's silence, Plato's myth, and two thousand years of better instruments until Kepler agreed to be the witness. Ideas are patient and promiscuous. The only exclusive claim you ever hold on one is execution, and the clock on that claim is always running. I build the way I do because I've watched ideas I slept on wake up in other hands.

</section>
