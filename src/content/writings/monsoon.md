<section class="part part--front" aria-label="Prelude">
  <p class="part-lede">Phoenix teaches drought as the default sky. The monsoon arrives as an event: the dust wall first, then lightning, then rain that the ground swallows in minutes. Farther north the rain is a climate, and the green there is permanent.</p>
</section>

<section class="part" aria-label="I">
  <p class="part-num">I.</p>

I was born a fish. Full moon, the Fish on one side of the sky and the Maiden on the other, and the first thing the world did was put me in the desert. Phoenix as a boy, where the sky is not your friend and not your enemy, just absent, and you build a whole life around the absence. Then a valley up the Gila for my teens, a river with a name and mostly no water in it, settled by my own blood five generations back. A fish raised in a dry riverbed and taught to call it home. Shade is a strategy. Water is a decision. You do not count on anything coming from above.

</section>

<section class="part" aria-label="II">
  <p class="part-num">II.</p>

Then every summer, for a few weeks, the sky remembers it exists. The dust wall comes first, a mile high, then the lightning, then rain so hard the ground can't take it and the streets run brown. Violent, brief, gone by morning, and you stand in the steam and understand that you just watched the whole year's grace arrive in forty minutes. That's how I used to love. That's how I used to work. That's how she used to reach me. Drought, drought, drought, flood.

</section>

<section class="part" aria-label="III">
  <p class="part-num">III.</p>

A fish can live in that. It can't thrive in it. I'm done building for absence. The move north is the plain version of a thing I've said six ways on this page already: I want to live where the rain is a climate and not an event. Where green isn't a miracle. Where you don't have to earn the water, and the thing that comes down on you is something you didn't make. The desert taught me to carry weight, to run, and to turn around for proof. Water teaches the only lesson left, which is to be still inside it. Cosmic consideration. Divine patience. Faith in the path. The fish goes home: not back down the dry bed, but north, under a sky that rains as a habit, into water deep enough that it can stop swimming and still be carried.

</section>
