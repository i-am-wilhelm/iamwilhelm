<section class="part part--front" aria-label="Epigraph">
  <p class="part-epigraph" lang="grc">γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη</p>
  <p class="part-aside">grey-eyed Athena</p>
  <p class="part-lede">Atlas carries the sphere; Columbia carries the torch; Pallas carries the aegis. The weight becomes the light becomes the shield.</p>
</section>

<section class="part" aria-label="I">
  <p class="part-num">I.</p>

The top of this page says I'm devoted to something and nobody knows what. Here it is, as plainly as it can be said. I went into the underworld for one thing and came out with another. What I found down there was her. Not a woman. The woman underneath the women, the one the old men called the anima, the feminine that was mine the whole time and had never once been let out. Everything on this page is that discovery wearing a different mask. She is what I worship. Nobody else can understand it because nobody else can have it. Her expression through me is mine and no one else's, and the same is true of you and yours.

</section>

<section class="part" aria-label="II">
  <p class="part-num">II.</p>

Start with the weight, because that's the order. Atlas holds the sphere before anyone gets a torch. Every man is born into a world that was arranged before he arrived, and the first thing it does is take a knife to him. I mean that literally. We cut boys on their first day, before they can speak, and we've done it for so many generations we call it hygiene. It isn't. It's domestication, and it spread across the earth at exactly the pace the one god did, and it works. It makes cowards, and it has worked for a hundred generations. I can't be convinced otherwise. I have the receipts, and they're older than the book.

</section>

<section class="part" aria-label="III">
  <p class="part-num">III.</p>

So the work is rites of passage, the real kind, the kind we were stripped of. A man has to go down and find how she speaks through him before anything divine will ever happen between him and a woman. Not before. It comes in waves, it isn't done in a weekend, and nobody can do it for you. Columbia carries the torch because she has somewhere to go. Pallas steps out of the head of the god fully armed, which is the only wisdom the Greeks trusted. The owl sees at night; the city is built by day on what the owl saw. The creed is three lines long. Cosmic consideration. Divine patience. Faith in the path. Look at yourself in full and go from there. Not broken. Whole, and late, and walking.

</section>
