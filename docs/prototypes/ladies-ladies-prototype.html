Ladies, Ladies

Minutes of a hearing before the Bureau of Coincidence

Drawn in eleven functions on graph paper. The points are yours to move.

drag the point
Functions on the record

    §1 · y = 0

    Call to order

    1.1The Bureau of Coincidence convened at the hour of 2° Aquarius, the Presiding Officer taking the bench. The bench is a line. The Bureau notes for the record that it has always been a line.

    1.2Present: the Clerk, recording. The Author, respondent, appearing on his own behalf, having declined counsel on the grounds that "counsel would only make it worse." Seven complainants, represented by their exhibits. One intervenor, who had not yet arrived and whose arrival the Bureau did not anticipate.

    1.3The matter: whether the poems published by the Author under the headings The Keeper and the Flame, Pharmakon, and The Unseen One Pleads His Case bear resemblance to persons living, dead, or formerly dating the Author, and if so, whether said resemblance is, as his standard notice claims, purely coincidental.

    1.4The Clerk read the standard notice into the record. The Author mouthed along.

    §2 · a form, four hatched fields

    The standard notice

    2.1"The works in question are works of imagination. Any resemblance to actual persons, names, settings, hours of the night, windows, flames, fruit, or turns of phrase is purely coincidental."

    2.2The Presiding Officer asked the Author whether he stood by the notice. The Author stated that he did.

    2.3Asked whether he had written the notice himself, the Author stated that he had, "at about three in the morning, which I would like entered as a mitigating circumstance."

    2.4Entered.

    §3 · x = 3 · a beam strikes it at one point

    Testimony of the Window

    3.1The Window was sworn. The Window testified that it is a vertical line of restricted domain and has never been a window.

    3.2Asked why a complainant recognized it as the window of a specific apartment in a specific city in a specific winter, the Window stated: "A line of light hit me at one point. One point. That is what a coincidence is. Two things that happen to share a coordinate."

    3.3The Bureau asked the Author to respond.

    I love from the other side of a window. I've said it in verse and I'll say it under oath. The window in the poem is the one I've been standing at since before I knew her name, or anyone's. If a real window happened to be where I was standing, that is the window's business.

    3.4The Clerk notes that the Author's voice changed on the word her and resumed its normal register on the word business.

    3.5The Presiding Officer invited the reader to drag the point. The flare went out. The Bureau entered the result: resemblance terminated at the reader's hand.

    §4 · a parabola, leading coefficient negative

    Testimony of the Flame

    4.1The Flame was sworn. The Flame stated that it is a parabola with a negative leading coefficient and declined to say more without a lamp present.

    4.2A complainant alleges that the Flame went out at the exact hour a certain conversation ended. The Flame testified that it goes out at the vertex, that every parabola does, and that it cannot be held responsible for conversations that also have vertices.

    4.3The Author, asked to respond:

    I kept that flame for a long time. Keepers don't choose the weather. The poem is about the keeping. If someone recognized the hour, it's because hours like that are more common than any of us were told.

    4.4The Presiding Officer asked whether the Keeper was himself. The Author said, "Your Honor, the Keeper is a man alone with a light he didn't make, waiting for a ship he can't signal. I'd prefer the Bureau not look into it."

    4.5The Bureau did not look into it. The reader may move the vertex and watch the inner flame return.

    §5 · a circle containing six points

    Testimony of the Pomegranate

    5.1The Pomegranate was sworn. It gave its name as an equation and asked that the record reflect it contains exactly six points, "which is a number, Your Honor, and numbers belong to everyone."

    5.2A complainant alleges the six seeds correspond to six specific things said over six specific months. The Pomegranate testified that six is six; that it has no months; and that if a reader counted to six in their own life and arrived somewhere painful, "the counting was theirs."

    5.3The Author asked permission to address the Pomegranate directly. Granted.

    You were the hardest one. I wrote the Unseen One pleading because I know the plea. Six seeds is the number you can eat and still be allowed to leave for half the year. I'm not sorry it landed. I'm sorry where.

    5.4The Pomegranate did not respond. The Clerk notes that the sixth seed remained lit for the remainder of the hearing. Move any seed and the fruit drains to graph paper.

    §6 · the form returns, fields filled

    The Author's intake form

    6.1The Presiding Officer reviewed the Author's intake form and noted the entry under Ruling Planet.

    6.2The Author confirmed the entry. "Venus. Twelfth house. In exile, if you want the technical word. She runs the chart from behind a wall and I write what comes through."

    6.3Asked whether this was offered as a defense, the Author said it was offered as a description. "Fantasy is going to keep happening, Your Honor. I'd rather the Bureau know in advance."

    6.4The Bureau entered the condition as pre-existing and noted that it did not, by itself, explain the window.

    6.5At this point the light under the page left the exhibits and moved to the empty margin, and the Clerk records that the room became warmer by a degree no instrument present could measure.

    §7 · a sine wave; a circle; a cross beneath it

    Intervenor

    7.1The intervenor entered without being announced. The Clerk records her as Aphrodite, appearing in her capacity as the Author's ruling planet, "and because she was in the area."

    7.2Asked on what basis she intervened, she stated: "Every resemblance in this room is mine. I put the light on the pane. I set the hour of the flame. I counted the seeds. He wrote down what I did. You are trying a stenographer."

    7.3Asked whether the resemblances were, then, intentional:

    Nothing I do is intentional. I am the goddess of things falling together. A face and a poem. A hand and a hand. He stood at a window because I stood him there. If a woman stood on the other side, that is what windows are for.

    7.4The Author asked to be heard.

    She's not wrong. But I'd like the record to show that I stood there willingly. Every time.

    7.5The Clerk notes that the intervenor looked at the Author for a period the minutes cannot record, and that the beam on the pane, the vertex of the flame, and the sixth seed were all lit at once, the only time in the Bureau's history that every exhibit has flared together.

    7.6The Presiding Officer asked the intervenor to state, for the record, the Greek for coincidence.

    §8 · two curves cross the page and meet twice

    The word

    8.1The intervenor stated: "σύμπτωμα. That which falls together. Aristotle uses it for a chance meeting. The physicians use it for what the body shows when something is wrong underneath."

    8.2The Presiding Officer asked whether the Bureau understood her correctly: that the Greek word for coincidence is the Greek word for symptom.

    8.3"You have understood me. When he writes that a resemblance is purely coincidental, he is writing, in the older language, that it is purely symptomatic. Of what, I leave to the Bureau."

    8.4The Author, asked whether he wished to amend his standard notice in light of this testimony, stated that he did not. "It's accurate either way, Your Honor. That's the trouble with it."

    §9 · two concentric circles, stamped

    Findings and order

    9.1The Bureau finds that the works bear resemblance to persons living, dead, and formerly dating the Author.

    9.2The Bureau finds that every such resemblance consists of two things sharing a coordinate, that the reader was able to terminate each resemblance by moving a single point, and that the resemblances were therefore coincidental within the meaning of the standard notice.

    9.3The Bureau further finds, on the intervenor's testimony, that the resemblances were also symptomatic, and that the two findings do not conflict.

    9.4The Author is certified coincidental and released into the custody of his ruling planet, who indicated she had him already.

    9.5The Bureau notes that the Author's condition is pre-existing and recurring, that further works are anticipated, and that each will be stamped on arrival.

    §10 · one point remains

    The Bureau takes commissions

    10.1The Bureau wishes it known that coincidences may also be arranged on purpose.

    10.2If you would like a poem that is about you, with your window, your hour, and your name on the intake form, the Author takes commissions. The resemblance will be intentional, certified as such, and yours to keep. Half to start, half at launch.

    10.3The intervenor asked to be listed as a reference. Granted.

    Arrange a coincidence or write: wilhelm@backgold.com

    Or drag the point and see what falls together.
