    The life, death and afterlife of John McAfee, told twice: once as the Record has it, and once as the believers do.

    Prologue

    The Wonderman

    1784 · 2021Two deaths on paper
    P.1Eckernförde, 27 February 1784

    A man known as the Count of Saint Germain dies in a converted factory in Eckernförde, in the Duchy of Schleswig. His patron, Prince Charles of Hesse-Kassel, had handed him the building in 1779 for his alchemy. The parish register of St. Nicolai records the burial.

    No one ever learned his real name or his true age. Voltaire, writing to Frederick the Great, called him “a man who never dies, and who knows everything.”

    P.2After the burial

    Five years after his recorded death, people claimed to see him in Paris. Theosophists Annie Besant and C.W. Leadbeater later said they had met him. In 1930 a mining engineer named Guy Ballard hiked Mount Shasta and came down saying Saint Germain had appeared to him. Ballard founded a religion on the encounter, and it still holds pageants on the mountain.

    The burial entry was the least interesting document he ever left behind.

    P.3Brians 2, 23 June 2021Tell No. 1

    John McAfee, 75, is found dead in his cell at Brians 2, a prison outside Barcelona. He had been held there for eight months. Spain’s National Court had just approved his extradition to the United States on tax charges. Catalan officials said everything pointed to suicide. A prison source told the AP that he usually shared the cell but was alone when he died.

    A man who spent forty years building exits was alone in a cell built for two. Ask who needed him alone.

    P.4The right armTell No. 2

    Nineteen months earlier, on 30 November 2019, McAfee posted a photo of a fresh tattoo on his right bicep that read $WHACKD. The caption ended: “If I suicide myself, I didn’t. I was whackd. Check my right arm.” $WHACKD was also the name of a crypto token he was promoting at the time.

    He told us, in ink, on the one part of his body the coroner would have to look at. Even the warning was for sale. That’s how you know it was really him.

    $WHACKD
    Two men who lived under borrowed names and impossible stories. Two deaths on paper. Two legends that refused to stay in the ground.This is the story of the second one.

    That’s the official version. Want the other one?

    Act I

    The Antidote

    1945–1994Escape #1: a fatherEscape #2: his own company
    I.1The GI baby

    John David McAfee is born on 18 September 1945 on a U.S. Army base in Cinderford, in England’s Forest of Dean. His father is an American soldier and his mother is English. The family settles in Salem, Virginia.

    He is born on a military base in an ancient forest, belonging fully to neither country. He spent his whole life that way, a citizen of somewhere else.

    I.2The father

    His father, a road surveyor and an abusive alcoholic, took his own life when John was 15. Decades later McAfee told Wired: “Every day I wake up with him.”

    The first escape wasn’t his. It was done to him, and it taught him that the man in charge can vanish in a single moment.

    I.3Inside the machine

    He earned a math degree from Roanoke College in 1967. He started a doctorate in Louisiana and was thrown out over an affair with an undergraduate. From 1968 he worked as a programmer at NASA’s Institute for Space Studies in New York, then at Univac, Xerox, Booz Allen Hamilton and Lockheed. He was drinking heavily and using drugs along the way, and he later got sober through AA.

    NASA, then defense contractors. The man who later swore the government wanted him dead started out inside its machine. He knew where the exits were, because he’d helped wire the building.

    I.4Brain

    In 1986 the first PC virus, Brain, spreads by floppy disk. At Lockheed, McAfee becomes obsessed with it. In 1987 he starts handing out a virus scanner as shareware from his bulletin board, on the honor system: pay if it helps. By 1989 it’s a company, McAfee Associates, and in 1990 he quits Lockheed to run it.

    There’s a rumor every antivirus company has heard, that the people selling the cure write the disease. No one ever proved it about McAfee, and no one ever stopped saying it.

    I.5The first prophecyTell No. 3

    The Michelangelo virus was set to trigger on 6 March 1992. McAfee told reporters that up to five million PCs could be wiped. The real damage was far smaller. That October his company went public and raised $42 million.

    He saw the panic coming and sold the cure into it. He understood early that fear is the real operating system.

    I.6Escape #2

    In 1994 he walks away from the company that bears his name, with a stake worth about $100 million.

    He shed his name like a skin and left it running on other people’s machines. You have probably tried to uninstall it.

    The five million

    McAfee’s Michelangelo estimate went around the world. A colleague who worked with him at the time later remembered the exchange differently.

    Prophet, salesman, or both?

    Act II

    The Alchemist

    1994–2012Fortune lost, or hidden
    II.1The Wonderman years

    With his fortune he buys land in Colorado, New Mexico and Hawaii. He runs a yoga retreat. He gets hooked on aerotrekking, flying open-cockpit ultralights at treetop height across the desert Southwest with a crew of pilots called the Sky Gypsies, and he builds them an airfield in New Mexico.

    Saint Germain had his castles and his laboratories. McAfee had runways in the desert where no one could see who landed.

    II.2The vanishing fortuneTell No. 4

    In 2009 he told The New York Times that the crash had wiped out all but $4 million of a $100 million fortune, and he auctioned off property. A novice aerotrekker had died in a 2006 crash, and a multimillion-dollar wrongful-death suit was hanging over him. McAfee later told Gizmodo that the Times figure was “not very accurate at all.”

    A man announces he’s broke right before he leaves the country. That isn’t a confession. It’s a cover story. Saint Germain always seemed to have money too, and no one ever found out where it came from.

    II.3The jungle laboratory

    He moves to Belize in 2008. He gives the national coast guard a boat worth more than $1 million and makes donations to the police. In 2010 he launches QuorumEx, a venture to develop antibiotics from rainforest plants. He sets up a compound near Orange Walk Town and a beach house on Ambergris Caye.

    The alchemist builds his last laboratory. Plants go in, and something comes out that the authorities will call a drug lab.

    II.4The raid

    At dawn on 30 April 2012, Belize’s Gang Suppression Unit raids the Orange Walk compound looking for a meth lab. They seize firearms, shoot one of his dogs, and hold him for about 14 hours. He is released without charge. McAfee says it’s punishment for refusing to pay off a local politician.

    Gizmodo reported that when police burst in, he came out of his bedroom with a 17-year-old Belizean girl. Reporters describe his household as armed guards and very young women.

    The empire had found him. From here on, every move he makes is a countermove.

    Act III

    The Vanishing

    Nov–Dec 2012Escape #3: a country and a murder inquiry
    III.111 November 2012

    Gregory Faull, 52, an American expat living next to McAfee on Ambergris Caye, is found shot dead in his home. Faull had complained about McAfee’s dogs, which had been poisoned shortly before. Police call McAfee a “person of interest.” He is never charged. A Faull family representative said the killing of a well-liked man was getting lost in the circus.

    McAfee had his own theory. The killers came for him and got the wrong house.

    III.2Under the sand

    When police came to his beach house, McAfee told Wired, he dug a shallow trench, buried himself in the sand and breathed through a cardboard box over his head for hours.

    This is his first rehearsal. He buries the body and keeps the mind breathing.

    III.3The man of many faces

    He goes on the run with his girlfriend, Samantha Vanegas, and blogs from hiding. He says he dyed his hair and beard, stained his teeth and stuck chewed gum in his gums to fatten his face. He says he posed as a drunk German tourist and as a Guatemalan street vendor. Belize’s prime minister calls him “extremely paranoid, even bonkers.”

    Saint Germain crossed Europe under a string of names and titles. McAfee crossed Belize under a string of faces. Neither man was ever quite the person standing in front of you.

    B.1Real name: unknownBeliever only
    The drunk German tourist. The Guatemalan street vendor. The grey-haired stranger. Gum in the cheeks. Stained teeth. Anyone but John McAfee.

    Saint Germain. Real name: unknown. Born 1691 or 1712, depending on who you ask.

    III.4The metadataTell No. 5

    On 3 December 2012, Vice posts a photo of its editor with McAfee. The iPhone image still carries its GPS tag. Within hours, people online place him at a resort near Río Dulce, Guatemala. McAfee first blogs that he faked the location data, then admits the next day that he really is in Guatemala. Vice later publicly wondered whether it had been played.

    The most famous antivirus name on earth was undone by a geotag. Or this was practice: plant false data, deny it, un-deny it, and watch how fast the world believes each version.

    III.5The heart · the precedentTell No. 6

    Guatemala arrests him on 5 December and turns down his asylum request. With deportation back to Belize looming, he’s rushed to hospital, and his lawyer says he has had two mild heart attacks. McAfee later tells the AP he didn’t think it was a heart attack. He tells the Miami New Times that he faked it to buy his lawyer time. On 12 December he’s deported to Miami, not Belize.

    Hold onto this one. In 2012 John McAfee faked a medical emergency in custody to change where the state sent his body, and it worked. That’s not a theory. It’s on his résumé.

    III.6The judgment

    In 2019 a Florida court ordered McAfee to pay $25 million to Faull’s estate in a wrongful-death suit. He said publicly that he wouldn’t pay.

    Even the verdict couldn’t find him.

    Act IV

    The Sovereign

    2013–2020Escape #4: the reach of the United States
    IV.1Uninstall

    In June 2013 he posts “How to Uninstall McAfee Antivirus.” He’s in a smoking jacket, surrounded by women, reading out hate mail about the software that carries his name, and the video gets millions of views. In January 2014 Intel, which had bought McAfee for $7.68 billion in 2011, says it will drop the McAfee name. He says publicly that he’s delighted. Around the same time he marries Janice Dyson, whom he met in Miami.

    He tried to delete himself from his own legacy, and it didn’t take. The brand came back. So does he.

    IV.2The candidate

    He runs for president with the Libertarian Party in 2016 and finishes third at the convention with about 14% of the first-ballot delegates. In 2018 he announces a 2020 run. By January 2019 he says that campaign will be run “in exile,” from international waters. That summer his campaign headquarters is a yacht moored at Marina Hemingway outside Havana.

    An empire’s most wanted man runs to lead it, from a boat it can’t board.

    IV.3The prophet of coin

    In 2017 he bets that Bitcoin will hit $500,000 within three years, and promises a grotesque act on national TV if it doesn’t. He later raises the target to $1 million by the end of 2020. In January 2020 he calls the whole thing “a ruse to onboard new users.”

    Federal prosecutors later charge that from late 2017 through 2018 he and his bodyguard Jimmy Gale Watson Jr. pumped coins on Twitter and took undisclosed payments, more than $13 million in all. The SEC put the undisclosed ICO payments at more than $23 million.

    He told you his prophecies were bait. So which of his other statements were bait too? The tattoo? The tweets from the cell? The death?

    IV.4The Great Mystery

    In January 2019 he says he hasn’t paid taxes in years and is fleeing the IRS. He and Janice live aboard a yacht named The Great Mystery with guards and dogs. In July 2019 the Dominican Republic detains the group after finding high-caliber weapons, ammunition and military-style gear aboard. They’re released four days later, and he’s put on a plane to Europe.

    You can’t make this up, and you don’t have to. The ship was really called The Great Mystery.

    IV.5The dead man’s switchTell No. 7

    In June 2019 he claims to hold more than 31 terabytes of incriminating data on official corruption, set to go to the press if he’s arrested or disappears. In January 2020 he says it’s on a check-in switch that triggers twice a day. For scale, the entire Panama Papers leak was 2.6 terabytes.

    He stopped checking in on 23 June 2021, and the files never came out. A dead man’s switch that never fires can mean only one thing: someone is still checking in.

    IV.6The departure gate

    On 30 November 2019 he posts the $WHACKD tattoo (see P.4). On 3 October 2020, Spanish police arrest him at Barcelona airport as he boards a flight to Istanbul. The U.S. tax indictment for 2014 to 2018 is unsealed two days later.

    Escape #4 ends at a departure gate. For the first time in eight years he doesn’t make it out, or so the Record says.

    Act V

    The Fifth Escape

    2020–2025Escape #5: the open question
    V.1Letters from Brians 2

    From prison, McAfee keeps tweeting through his team. On 15 October 2020 he writes that he’s content, he has friends and the food is good. He adds that if he’s ever found dead “a la Epstein,” it will be no fault of his. In May 2021 he writes about the small patch of blue sky he can see over the yard walls. In March 2021 federal prosecutors in Manhattan add crypto-fraud charges to the tax case.

    He’s writing his alibi in public, one tweet at a time.

    V.2The ruling

    On 15 June 2021 he appears by video at his extradition hearing in Madrid. The National Court approves extradition on tax charges covering 2016 to 2018, after dropping seven of the ten counts. His lawyer says they’re preparing an appeal. His pinned tweet from 16 June says his crypto is gone and his assets are seized. It ends: “I regret nothing.”

    The last trick in any escape act is convincing the audience the act is over.

    V.323 June 2021

    He’s found dead in his cell that day. His Spanish lawyer, Javier Villalba, says the family is stunned and that he “had not said goodbye.” Janice McAfee says he wasn’t suicidal and blames U.S. authorities.

    The timing, the empty cell, the tattoo, the tweet. Any one of them is a coincidence. All four together is a plan.

    V.4QTell No. 8

    Within half an hour of the first English-language reports, his Instagram account, which others ran while he was in jail, posts one image: a black capital Q on white, with no caption. Within hours the account is gone. A rumor spreads that the image file hides a cryptographic key, and the user who started it later concedes it was probably Instagram’s own code. #JohnMcAfeeDidntKillHimself trends.

    One letter, no caption, then deletion. If you wanted to start a myth with a single keystroke, this would be the keystroke.

    V.5The voices

    By July 2021, several Telegram channels claim to be McAfee. The largest passes 125,000 subscribers.

    Saint Germain had his disciples on Mount Shasta. McAfee had his Telegram channels.

    V.6The morgueTell No. 9

    The autopsy finds suicide, and a court in Martorell closes the case in February 2022. The family appeals and the body stays in a Spanish morgue. In April 2023 Janice posts that it has been 670 days with no remains and no autopsy report. In September 2023 the court rejects the final appeal, writing that there is “not a single element of suspicion.” In October 2023 it authorizes release of the body. In November 2023 she tells Cointelegraph she still just wants to see his body herself and know it really happened.

    For more than two years, the only people who could vouch for the body were the people holding it. The man who once faked a heart attack to choose where the state sent him had no say this time.

    670Days without remains or an autopsy report, as of April 2023
    V.7The call from TexasTell No. 10

    Netflix releases Running with the Devil: The Wild World of John McAfee on 24 August 2022. In it, his Belize ex-girlfriend Samantha Herrera says that about two weeks after his death she got a call from Texas. She says the caller said it was John, that he’d paid people to pretend he was dead, and that only three people knew. The director said he couldn’t make sense of her claim. Janice rejected it and said she wished it were true, but that John wouldn’t be hiding in Texas.

    Read his widow’s answer again. She didn’t say he was dead. She said he wasn’t in Texas.

    V.8Resurrection, 23 January 2025Tell No. 11

    After years of near-silence, @officialmcafee, which still has more than a million followers, posts: “I’m back with AIntivirus. An AI version of myself.” A Solana meme coin launches alongside a “chat with John” bot. Janice says she’s behind it, as a tribute. Crypto figures call it a grift, and X suspends the AIntivirus account.

    In 1930, Saint Germain spoke through a mining engineer on Mount Shasta. In 2025, McAfee spoke through a language model. Every immortal eventually gets a channeler.

    The body

    More than two years in a morgue. An autopsy report his widow says she never saw. We found no solid reporting on what happened to the remains after release was authorized.

    If you were going to fake a death, how long would you need the paperwork to stay closed?
