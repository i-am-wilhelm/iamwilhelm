A monthly column · No. 1 · the stapler

The Paranoiac-Critical Method, Applied

Dalí's recipe for turning a thing into a second thing. One ordinary object a month, walked all the way to the picture.

SCROLL ↓
I · The object

The rule: one object from the desk. This month, the stapler. Look at it until it stops being a stapler. Dalí called this inducing the delusion while keeping the critical faculty awake. Let the associations run; keep the hand steady.

The first thing it stops being is a tool. It becomes a hinge with two arms, and a hinge with two arms is a jaw.

That is the whole method in one move. You just did it.

II · The chain

Jaw. Open it. The anvil is the lower jaw, the magazine is the upper, and the whole thing is long and low and hinged at the back. The chain goes where it wants: crocodile.

Stay with the crocodile a second. It eats paper. It lives on a desk the way the Nile one lives on a bank: motionless until something passes.

Now the staple itself. A tiny Π. Two legs and a lintel. That is a doorway. Every staple you have ever driven is a doorway closed over a stack of pages.

A thousand doorways in a row is a colonnade. A colonnade with a roof is a temple. The thing in the crocodile's mouth is a temple made of staples.

And the word. Religion, by one old etymology, is religare: to bind back, to bind together. A stapler is a small machine for binding together. The chain closes on itself.

III · The image

Now render both readings so neither one loses. A stapler on a desk, drawn plainly. A crocodile with a temple in its mouth and worshippers walking in, drawn plainly. The same lines.

The hinge pin is the sun. The anvil is the floor. The staples are the columns. Nobody in the picture is surprised.

IV · The method, written down

Pick an object you touch every day. Name what it is made of mechanically: hinge, arm, lever, spring. Every mechanism has an animal and a building hiding in it. Follow one until a word arrives.

Then draw it twice at once. The precision is the point. Dalí's phrase for it was the most imperialist fury of precision, and he meant it: a sloppy double image is only a blur.

Next month's object is yours to nominate. The desk is full of them.

The desk is a zoo
and a city.

The paranoiac-critical method is a generator, and generators are cheap; the chain from stapler to crocodile to temple took four minutes. The craft is in the last step, where both readings have to survive the same lines. That is where the month goes. Nominate an object and it gets the treatment: mechanism, animal, building, word, picture.

No. 2 will be whatever you leave on the desk.
