Seen · No. 11 · A scrolly on gold, cubes and a borrowed halo

Why Klimt Went to Ravenna

In December 1903 a painter from Vienna stood in a sixth-century church and looked at a wall of gold. Four years later he painted a kiss inside it.

I · The cube

The church is San Vitale, in Ravenna, finished in the year 547. Its walls are pictures made of small cubes of glass and stone, pressed by hand into mortar.

Each cube is one color. Step back, and they add up to an empress with a crown, pearls and a halo. Slide the cube size and watch her come into focus.

It is the trick behind a letter on a phone screen: small squares, blended by your eye. Here you could reach out and touch them.

II · The tilt

The gold cubes are glass too. A leaf of real gold, far thinner than a hair, is sealed between two layers of glass.

The makers set gold cubes at slightly different angles. Each one is a tiny mirror aimed its own way.

Move the candle. A cube flashes when its tilt sends the light to your eye, and dims when it does not. The figures stay still. The gold never does.

A church lit by candles would have flickered like this all day.

III · No place

Painters who want depth follow one rule: draw things smaller as they get farther away. Floors tilt back, shadows fall, and the picture becomes a room.

The gold wall has no use for that rule. In San Vitale the emperor and his court stand in a row, facing you. Overlapping feet are the only clue to who stands in front.

Slide from room to gold. The floor goes, the shadows go, and nobody is near or far. Klimt saw how much brighter the gold looks when the picture stays flat.

IV · The painter

Gustav Klimt already knew gold. His father engraved it for a living, and Klimt had been putting gold leaf in his pictures since about 1898.

In December 1903 he studied the mosaics of San Vitale. A fellow painter who stood there with him wrote that they made an immense, decisive impression.

Back in Vienna he kept faces and hands soft and real, and turned everything else into flat gold filled with signs: blocks, circles, spirals, eyes. Try them on the cloak.

Each pattern is one small rule, repeated until the shape is full.

In The Kiss his robe carries hard-edged shapes and hers carries flowers. The gold is real: gold leaf, with silver and platinum.

V · The halo

In Ravenna, a disc of gold behind a head marked the emperor and the empress. Gold was the sign of something above ordinary life.

Klimt kept the gold and changed what it crowned. Slide the halo. It grows past the head, past the shoulders, until it holds two people.

One writer on Klimt judged his golden portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer closer to a religious icon than to a society portrait.

Ravenna gave him the parts: cubes, mirrors and a wall with no near or far. He used them to make a kiss look as if it would last.

He kept
the gold.

A church used gold to lift an empress out of time. A painter borrowed it for two people in a meadow.

Next time you see gold in a picture, ask what it is crowning.
