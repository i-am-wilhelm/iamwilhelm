0.1General notes

Where the money drops

A fall line is the ledge where a river drops. Mill towns grew along it, because falling water does work once a wheel sits under it. Family money has one too. It arrives at every death.

Two stamps mark what follows. On the record is documented history and current law. As taught is the method the way planners and insurance agents sell it. The family's books are closed to researchers,1 so the stamps matter.

1.1Sheet 1

The spring

Paychecks arrive at the spring. Whatever outlasts the month collects in the pond: the house, the savings, the retirement accounts.

Every part downstream moves water. This is the only part that makes any.

1.2On the record

The drop

At death the pond goes over the ledge. With nothing built below, it lands on rock.

A court supervises the estate in public, and settlement commonly costs 3% to 8% of it.8 If minor children have no named guardian, a judge chooses one. What survives is handed over outright.

1.3On the record

The catch basin

The first part is a revocable living trust, plus a will that pours stragglers into it. You run it while you live. Your successor takes over at the drop, and the water skips the court. A lawyer-drafted plan typically runs $1,500 to $4,000.9

Know its limits. It saves no estate tax, stops no creditor, and catches only what you retitle into it.9

1.4On the record

The charter

The second part is free. Bolt the rules to the basin wall: what this money is for, who decides, what gets lent and what gets given.

The Rockefellers gather twice a year, often more than a hundred in one room, and bring members into the meetings at 21.7 The household edition is one page and one dinner a year.

1.5On the recordAs taught

The tank

The third part is life insurance that pays the trust. A thin pipe of premiums fills a tank with one valve, and the valve opens at death.

A rented tank. Large, cheap, and it expires. In one Consumer Reports comparison the term policy cost $660 a year.10 Critics of the method buy this one and invest the difference.

An owned tank. Permanent, with a sight glass of cash value to borrow against. The same comparison priced it at $6,760 a year.10 The method as taught runs on this one.5

1.6On the recordAs taught

The family bank

The fourth part is a gate and a return pipe. The trust lends to family on written terms, for purposes the charter names, and the loan flows back with interest.

The IRS posts a floor rate each month. For loans made in November 2026 it runs 4.67% to 5.52% by term, and loans of $10,000 or less are generally exempt.15 In the insurance edition, the borrowing happens against the policy's cash value.6

1.7As taught

The waterfall

Now run it. A parent dies, the valve opens, the basin fills. The trustee buys a tank on the next generation and keeps the gate working. Each drop refills the trust that funds the next.

One trust, a policy on every generation, a family that borrows from itself. That loop is the Rockefeller waterfall as it is sold.5

1.8On the record

Sheet 1, bill of materials

A will. A funded revocable trust. A one-page charter. Coverage that pays the trust. A loan ledger.

Nothing on this sheet touches estate tax, because the federal line sits at $15 million a person in 2026.13 Twelve states and D.C. draw their own lines lower: Oregon at $1 million, Massachusetts at $2 million, Washington at $3 million.14

2.1On the record

The source

Same drawing, bigger pipe. The Rockefeller spring was Standard Oil. The trusts that followed held shares in its successor companies, other investments and real estate.1

The plumbing on this sheet kept a fortune together. Oil made it.

2.2On the record

The toll gate

At this scale the fall line charges a toll. Federal estate tax takes 40% of everything above $15 million a person, $30 million for a married couple.13

A fortune that follows the natural channel pays at every generation.

2.3On the record

The dam

So John D. Rockefeller Jr. built above the gate. In 1934 he set up irrevocable trusts for his six children, and in 1952 a second set for his grandchildren.2 Chase administers them with a trust committee.1

Principal stays behind the wall. Income leaves through the outlet. The builder gives the water up for good, and that surrender buys the bypass.3

2.4On the record

The cornerstone

Congress later added a second toll on transfers that skip a generation. The 1934 trusts were poured first and were grandfathered.3

A dam built today rests on a different cornerstone, each person's $15 million generation-skipping exemption,13 in a state that lets a trust run for centuries or forever. South Dakota, Nevada, Delaware, Alaska and Wyoming are the usual sites.3

2.5On the recordAs taught

The tank, inside the wall

At estate scale the tank moves into an irrevocable life insurance trust. The trust buys the policy, so the payout lands outside the taxable estate. Premiums arrive as gifts within the $19,000 annual exclusion, and every gift needs a written notice to the beneficiaries.16

The sales version calls this tank the Rockefeller engine. The record shows oil stock and real estate, and one careful source says only that the family is “thought to use” the method.4

2.6On the record

The control house

A dam needs operators. The family office moved in 1933 to the 56th floor of 30 Rockefeller Plaza, and the door number, 5600, became its name. Investments, accounting, legal work and philanthropy ran through it, with nearly 400 people at its height.2

Trustees work the valves.

2.7On the record

The irrigation canal

One channel leaves the watershed on purpose. The Rockefeller Foundation, chartered in 1913, and the Rockefeller Brothers Fund, founded in 1940, carry water to fields the family will never own.

David Rockefeller Jr. told CNBC the family has no shared business to fight over. Shared values and giving do the binding.7

2.8On the record

Seven generations

Follow the cascade down. Seven generations and more than 200 descendants later, the water is still inside the works.19

The Vanderbilts built mansions at their fall line. The famous line says 120 of them gathered in 1973 without a millionaire in the room. That count is disputed.18 The Fifth Avenue mansions are gone either way.

3.1Inspection

Leak one, the owned tank

Whole life fills slowly. In the Consumer Reports comparison the guaranteed cash value earned about 1.5% a year,10 and cash value can take ten to fifteen years to catch up with premiums paid.2011

Borrow against it and interest accrues. An unpaid loan comes out of the death benefit. Fill the tank too fast in the first seven years and the loans become taxable.12

3.2Inspection

Leak two, the wall

An irrevocable trust keeps its own tax table. In 2026 trust income reaches the 37% bracket above $16,000. A single filer gets there at $640,600.17

The paperwork is structural. One estate firm calls missing gift notices the most common defect it sees in older insurance trusts, and a policy moved in within three years of death falls back into the estate.16 The builder never gets the water back.

3.3Inspection

Leak three, the spring

The largest leak sits at the top of Sheet 1. Every part on these drawings moves water. None of them makes any.

The Rockefellers had oil. A household has a paycheck, a business, a skill. Sheet 2 solves a tax most families will never owe. Sheet 1 is the one to build, and it starts at the spring.

Construction sequence

Build order for one household

Sheet 1, in the order the parts go in. Each line is also a question to bring to a licensed professional in your state.

  1. Name guardians and sign a will. The court stops choosing.
  2. Set up the revocable trust and retitle into it. House, accounts, beneficiary forms. An empty basin catches nothing.
  3. Point coverage at the trust. Price the rented tank and the owned tank side by side before signing either.
  4. Write the one-page charter. Then hold the dinner.
  5. Keep a loan ledger. A written note, a schedule, and at least the federal floor rate on anything over $10,000.
  6. Call the dam builders when the numbers say so. Your state's estate-tax line, or the federal one, is the trigger for irrevocable work.

This drawing is general education on U.S. federal rules as of November 2026. State law varies and the figures change every year. It is no substitute for an estate attorney and a fee-only planner licensed where you live.
