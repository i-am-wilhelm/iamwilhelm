General notes

Past the hundred-fathom line

A ship is on soundings while her lead line can still find bottom. Past the hundred-fathom line she is off soundings, and the chart goes white.

Depth Chart sounded the home harbor: five layers of money and a dollar line. Fall Line built the vessel: a trust that outlives its builder. This chart takes both out of port.

Black ink is law and data. Magenta marks hazards, the way a working chart prints them.

General notes

Two dials, one compass

Private and offshore get used as one word. They are two dials. East and west say where a thing's legal home is. North and south say who can see it and who can get in. Every quarter is occupied.

Stn 1Public · Onshore

Currency Roads

You can leave the dollar without leaving the dock. Currency trading runs $9.6 trillion a day, and the dollar sits on one side of 89% of it.1 One large U.S. broker lets a client hold eight currencies and trade twelve foreign markets direct. Another lists 28 currencies.2

Buys
Other currencies and foreign shares, inside U.S. custody.
Toll
Your usual tax forms. A euro balance in New York counts as domestic.9
Hazard
Leverage, capped at 50 to 1. At one U.S. dealer, 69% of retail accounts lost money in the first quarter of 2026.3
Stn 2Public · Both shores

Dollar Bank

Stablecoins look like open water. Depth Chart found 99.76% of them pegged to the dollar.4 The whole non-dollar market runs near $1.6 billion, and its largest token is a sanctioned ruble coin.5 Sailing off this bank means euro tokens under Europe's rulebook, tokenized gold, or bitcoin.

Toll
Every swap is a taxable sale. The IRS treats a stablecoin as property, and brokers report cost basis from 2026.6
Hazard
The issuer's off switch travels with the token. A peg can slip too: USDC touched 87 cents in March 2023.7
Stn 3Hazard

PFIC Reef

The first reef lies just past the shelf. Buy a fund domiciled abroad, a London-listed ETF or a Dublin index fund, and U.S. law calls it a passive foreign investment company. The default rule spreads your gain across every year you held it, taxes each slice at that year's top rate, and adds compounding interest.8

Passage
The same foreign stocks inside a U.S.-domiciled fund clear the reef.8
Toll
Form 8621, one for each fund, each year.8
Stn 4Public · Offshore

Port Account

A bank or brokerage account in another country. Location makes it foreign: a dollar account in London is foreign, a euro account in New York is domestic.9 It buys a second banking system and a second set of courts.

Toll
An FBAR once all foreign accounts together pass $10,000 on any day. Form 8938 from $50,000 for a single U.S. resident.9
Hazard
Willful failure to file can cost the greater of $165,353 or half the balance.9
Off forms
Real estate in your own name. Bullion held directly. A custodial metal account is reportable.10
Stn 5The sailor moves

Exit Roads

Some courses relocate the sailor. Puerto Rico gives bona fide residents 0% island tax on gains earned after the move, for decrees filed by December 31, 2026. File later and the rate is 4%.11 Abroad, an exclusion covers $132,900 of earned income in 2026.12

Toll
A U.S. citizen files on worldwide income from anywhere. Gains built before the move stay taxable.11
Hazard
Handing in the passport triggers an exit tax at $2 million of net worth, a line unchanged since 2008.12
Stn 6Private · Offshore

Trust Islands

Here Fall Line's basin moves to the Cook Islands, Nevis or Belize. A creditor has to sue there, within about two years, and prove intent to defraud beyond reasonable doubt. Local courts ignore foreign judgments. Setup starts near $15,000.13

Buys
Distance from a future lawsuit. Zero tax savings, since the IRS treats the settlor as owner.15
Toll
Forms 3520 and 3520-A. A missed one costs the greater of $10,000 or 35% of what moved.15
Hazard
Wreck, 1998. A U.S. judge jailed the Andersons for contempt while the money stayed in Rarotonga.14
Stn 7Private · Either shore

Policy Light

Fall Line's tank, at ocean scale. Private placement life insurance wraps a custom portfolio inside a policy, so gains compound untaxed and the death benefit passes tax-free. A Senate report counted at least $40 billion of it among a few thousand holders.18

Buys
A wrapper for heavily taxed assets. Entry runs to a few million dollars.18
Toll
The owner gives up investment control. The portfolio stays diversified.18
Hazard
A bill to end the tax treatment was filed in April 2026.18
Stn 8Private · Onshore

Dakota Sound

The course bends home. The United States never joined the account-sharing standard that more than 100 countries use, and the Tax Justice Network ranks it first in the world for financial secrecy. South Dakota has allowed perpetual trusts since 1983.17 Fall Line's dam already stands in this water.

Buys
Privacy from the public and a trust built to run for centuries, under U.S. law.
Toll
About 20 states allow self-settled protection trusts. Most keep the door open to divorce and support claims.16
Hazard
A transfer made after a claim exists can be unwound.16
Stn 9All four quarters

Off soundings

Now assemble the engine. The trust sits onshore, in the deepest home water. What it owns sits in every quarter: a brokerage holding several currencies, an account under a second flag, metal below the dollar line, a policy around the taxable parts. Each piece answers to a different switch.

Harry Schultz called this flag theory in the 1960s: passport, address and assets under separate flags.21 The trust engine does it on paper, and every piece of it is reported.

Tidal notes

Set and drift

Two currents cross this chart. One ebbs east. The dollar's share of world reserves has slid to 56.7%, and central banks bought a record 289 tonnes of gold in a single quarter of 2026.20

The other floods home. The dollar index stands near 103, up about 4% this year and at its highest since April 2025.19 Gold fell roughly a quarter between January and June.20 A course off the dollar is a position, and positions lose too.

Notices to marinersRead before sailing

Five corrections

Tax follows the citizen. Offshore changes the court and leaves the tax bill where it was.

The forms are the reef. Penalties out here are sized to the balance.

Timing is the hull. Built before trouble, it holds. Built after, it gets unwound.

Control is the anchor chain. Keep the power to bring money home and a judge can order you to use it.

Draft matters. Four stations take any boat. The islands cost five figures. The light takes millions.

Passage plan

Shortest draft first

The same course, in the order a household would sail it. Each line is a question to carry to a cross-border tax attorney before any money moves.

  1. Sound your own harbor. Run Depth Chart's three questions on everything you hold. Then add two from this chart: which flag is it under, and who can see it.
  2. Leave the dollar from the dock first. Currency and foreign shares inside a U.S. account add no new forms.
  3. Steer wide of foreign-domiciled funds. The same exposure through a U.S. fund clears the reef.
  4. Open the port account on purpose. Know the $10,000 line before the wire goes out, and file every year.
  5. Build the trust at home before looking abroad. Fall Line's basin and charter come first, and the strongest privacy law on this chart is domestic.
  6. Sail for the islands early or skip them. They protect against the lawsuit that has yet to happen. They need counsel on both shores and a settlor willing to let go of the controls.
  7. Treat every figure as perishable. The rates, thresholds and bills on this chart are dated November 2026.

Not for navigation. This chart is general education on U.S. federal rules as of November 2026, and cross-border mistakes carry penalties sized to the asset. It is no substitute for a tax attorney and a licensed adviser, and nothing on it recommends buying anything.
