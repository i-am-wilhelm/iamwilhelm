Nine Hundred Lights

Nine hundred apprentices, training to be their own flame keepers and standing watch on the shore for brigands. This is my endorsement of the Libertarian Leadership Academy, and the case that a party full of trained people stays in its own hands.

Michael Wilhelm

A bare flame

A slider moves the ship from one mile out to six. The light spreads as it travels. At one mile all of it arrives. At two miles a quarter arrives. At six miles about three percent arrives.

I · The flame

Liberty is a flame, and it has never burned low. Stand beside it at a convention, a county meeting or a kitchen table, and it is the brightest thing in the room.

Out past the room it thins fast. Slide the ship out. A voter three counties over sees a flicker.

A bright flame in a thin house draws company. Some come to keep the light. Some are brigands, and they come for the tower.

A room of nine hundred chairs

A slider sets how many of the nine hundred chairs are filled by keepers, shown in gold from the front of the room. Brigands, shown in red, fill empty chairs from the back. With 120 keepers, 121 brigands carry the room. Once keepers fill more than half the chairs, the empty chairs left over cannot hold an outside majority.

II · The room

Every party office is decided in a room. A county meeting, a state convention, a national floor. Whoever fills the chairs holds the tower.

Here is a room of nine hundred chairs with a hundred and twenty keepers in it. Watch what one boatload of brigands does with the empty ones.

That is the whole trick of a takeover from the outside: empty chairs and a head count. The speeches come later, from the podium, once the tower has changed hands.

Now slide the keepers up. Past half the room, the brigands have nowhere to sit. Recruitment fills the chairs. Training is why the people in them stay.

III · The focus

Filling chairs is half of it. A room of new arrivals, each on their own heading, is light without a lens. Slide the lamp and watch the rays cross, then fan out.

Training is the lens. It takes the same people and the same fire and sends them out level, side by side. An apprentice learns how to ask, how to keep a list, how to run a meeting, and how to lose a vote and stay.

This is the work of the Libertarian Leadership Academy, and it has my full endorsement. It trains candidates, campaign staff and organizers, online and in person, and its door is open on the same terms to anyone ready to lead.

Its goal for this cycle is on the wall: nine hundred candidates, at every level, nationwide. Nine hundred apprentices, each training to be the keeper of their own flame.

One level at a time
School board

A slider raises the lamp through five levels named for offices: school board, city council, county commission, statehouse and Congress. At each level the light is seen from farther out, from about ten miles at the lowest to about twenty-seven at the highest. The drawing exaggerates the curve of the sea.

IV · The climb

Keepers are made on the stairs. School board, city council, county commission, statehouse. Slide the lamp up a level and watch how far it carries.

Every campaign, win or lose, leaves a keeper behind. Someone who has knocked the doors, carried the petitions and balanced the books. Those are the people in the chairs when the brigands land.

The count is within reach. Ballotpedia lists 967 Libertarian candidates in 2018. Nine hundred campaigns, each with a handful of volunteers, is a few thousand people who earned their seat and know a stranger's pitch when they hear one.

A slider cuts the second lens into one to twelve rings. With one ring it is the same solid dome. With twelve it is a thin plate that throws the same beam at about a sixth of the weight. Small prisms at its edges catch stray rays and turn them level.

V · The glass

Keepers need a lens they can lift. The old one was a boulder: one subscription for the list, another for email, another for donations, another for the site, and data lost each time one lapsed.

Slide the rings. The same curve, cut thin, does the same work at a fraction of the weight. That is BackGold: the list, the outreach, the money, the site and the map on one plate, kept year over year, election over election.

A party that keeps its list keeps its people, and a party that keeps its people is hard to outnumber. The rest is craft: designers with taste and a hunger to carry the message all the way to the reader.

Each light keeps time
Rhythm

Two buttons choose the rhythm. As charted, the light sends two white flashes every 30 seconds and the sailor's count matches the chart. With the rhythm changed, the same tower sends three red flashes and the count no longer matches the chart. The animation runs faster than life.

VI · The signature

At night every light is a dot. Sailors tell one from another by its rhythm, and the rhythm is printed on the chart.

Here is a takeover seen from the water. Press Rhythm changed. Same tower, same flame, bright as ever. The count is wrong, and a ship steering by the chart finds the rocks.

Ours has been on the chart since 1971. Each person owns their own life. Trade is free. Peace is the policy.

Trained keepers know that count by heart, so the door can stand wide open. Everyone who comes for the light is welcome in the tower. The rabble-rousers of empire can find another hall to shout in.

The same room, lit
1 / 900

A slider lights from 1 to 900 lamps, scattered across thirty rows of thirty. Every lit lamp keeps the same rhythm of two flashes and a pause. A readout counts the lamps lit and the lamps still to go.

VII · The coast

Here is the same room. Nine hundred chairs, thirty rows of thirty. Slide the lights on.

Each one is an apprentice turned keeper. A neighbor who was recruited, trained and stood up where they live, tending their own flame and watching their own stretch of shore.

From the water, nine hundred lights read as a single coast. Brigands take towers. Nobody takes a coast.

Face forward.

The flame is as bright as it ever was. What guards it is people: recruited, trained and in their chairs.

The tools are on the bench and the number is on the wall. What is left is the climb, and everyone who makes it wins. The apprentice gets a craft, the party gets a coast, and the voter gets a light to steer by.

Where I stand

I came into this movement in 2016 as one of eight delegates Utah sent to Orlando, and I came back to it this year. Today I am cofounder and CEO of BackGold and vice president of the board at the Libertarian Leadership Academy.

The Academy opens its door to anyone ready to lead. What that training does for the Libertarian Party is my own read, and I stand on it: nine hundred apprentices, training to be their own flame keepers, guarding the shore against brigands.

My own post is the lantern room. I learned design just over a year ago, and I now have dozens of pages as beautiful and as unique as this one. I grind the glass and keep it clean.

I call it the long apprenticeship of keeping fire.
