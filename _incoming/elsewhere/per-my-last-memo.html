The five laws of human stupidity, confirmed this quarter at one pizza place, three offices, a four-way stop and your family group chat.

In 1976 a Berkeley economic historian named Carlo M. Cipolla had a short essay printed for his friends. The edition was private and numbered, under the imprint Mad Millers. It laid out five basic laws of human stupidity and argued them with graphs. It reads like the work of a man who had sat through one faculty meeting too many.

The essay got loose. Whole Earth Review reprinted it in 1987, an Italian edition followed in 1988, and the English original came out as a book in 2011. Cipolla died in 2000. The laws are still in force. You can confirm this in any comment section.

Regional is the office everybody reports to and nobody has met. This quarter Regional checked all five laws against its incident reports. Most of them come from Location 114, which is a pizza place. The rest come from places you already know: the office, the airport, the road, the group chat. Every law held.

Please read the findings, fill out the form at the end, and stop microwaving fish.

The First Law

There are more of them than you think

The law, in plain EnglishEverybody, every time, lowballs the number of stupid people on the loose.

Take any 114 people. Friday dinner rush at the pizza place, your office holiday party, Gate B22. It makes no difference.

How many of them are stupid? Give Regional a number. Use the slider. Be honest.

Adorable. The real number is higher.

Cipolla’s first law says it is always higher, for everyone, every time. That is why he never gave a number himself. Anything he wrote down would have been too low before the ink dried. He called the share σ and changed the subject.

Go ahead and raise your guess. Regional has raised its own five times this quarter, in Circulars 76-N through 76-R. Each one was correct for about a day.

Handwritten in the margin: 76-R is the one where he wrote “surely that’s all of them”

You already know this law. You relearn it every time you leave the house, and the proof is printed on everything you own.

The hair dryer says do not use while sleeping. The coffee cup says the coffee is hot. The bag of peanuts says it contains peanuts. Every warning label is a small plaque honoring a specific person, and somebody had to meet that person first.

Your guess runs low for two reasons. You built it out of people you picked: friends, the coworkers you eat lunch with, the one group chat you have not muted. Then you renew your license, or fly on a holiday weekend, or read the replies.

And you only count the ones you caught. You saw the guy who replied all. You missed the eleven people who replied all to tell him to stop replying all.

Tap anyone on the sheet, or pull a file. Everybody marked has one.

The Second Law

It can be anybody

The law, in plain EnglishWhether a person is stupid has nothing to do with anything else about them. Degrees, job, money, title. None of it helps.

The obvious move is to work out where the stupid people are and go stand somewhere else.

Regional tried. Regional sorted everybody eight ways. Press a button and watch the red line. It does not move.

Cipolla ran this at a university. Janitors, office staff, students, administrators, professors. The same share in every group, at the fancy schools and at the other kind.

Then he checked Nobel laureates. Same share. Somewhere tonight a Nobel Prize winner is pushing on a door marked PULL.

You have watched this law work. The surgeon who cannot beat the parking machine. The engineer who replied all. The man with two doctorates standing on the top rung of a ladder, the rung with the sticker on it.

A diploma certifies what somebody knows. It has no opinion about what they are about to do.

This is the law people fight hardest, because everybody has a column they were counting on.

Money does not fix it. That is a ninety-thousand-dollar truck parked across two compact spaces. Age does not fix it. Your politics do not fix it, and neither do theirs.

Being sure does not fix it either. Regional really thought that one would work.

And the columns have room for you. The laureate at the PULL door has company. You have pushed on that door, and you looked around afterward to see who saw.

Handwritten in the margin: Dale has a master’s. in ENGINEERING.

As a control, Regional sorted by sun sign. σ, twelve times. Mercury was not in retrograde. Regional checked.

Then by species. The dogs came back clean. The cats declined to participate.

The Third Law, called golden

Everybody loses, including the guy who did it

The law, in plain EnglishA stupid person is someone who costs other people something and gets nothing out of it, or even loses on the deal.

Everything a person does gets two numbers. One is what it did for them. The other is what it did for everybody else.

Plot the first one across and the second one up, and all of humanity sorts into four corners. It is the most useful graph Regional owns, and Regional owns a lot of graphs.

Three of the corners make sense. Someone fixed the printer. Good for her, good for the floor. Intelligent.

Someone gave up his Saturday to help a friend move a couch. Bad for him, great for the friend. Cipolla’s word is helpless, and he had clearly carried a couch.

And someone reclined his airplane seat. He gained two inches. The lady behind him lost exactly two. That is a bandit. You may hate him, and at least you understand him.

The fourth corner is the law. A man replied all to four hundred people. They lost a morning. He gained nothing.

A man in a lanyard cited a rule that does not exist, was asked to produce it, and could not. Now everyone is late and he is furious.

Nobody came out ahead, and nobody can tell you why it happened, least of all him. Cipolla called this one golden because it is the whole definition. Stupid is the lower left.

Handwritten in the margin: lower left = Dale’s corner

Once you know the corner, you see it on every commute. The driver who pulls into the intersection on a red and blocks it, so that nobody moves, himself included. The man who brake-checks a stranger on the freeway and is now also late.

Two neighbors who spent four thousand dollars on lawyers over six inches of hedge. The crowd that jams the gate before its row is called, so the whole plane boards slower, them too.

Nobody profits. That is how you tell it from crime.

Now the red diagonal. Right on the line sits the perfect bandit, who takes exactly what you lose. The seat recliner. The tip jar skimmer. Nothing was created and nothing was destroyed. It just changed seats.

Above the line, a bandit makes more than he costs. That is how lunch gets expensed and the deal still closes. Below the line he wrecks more than he gets. One hot lunch for him, one ruined afternoon for forty people. He microwaved the fish.

Then there is the far corner. Some people take everyone down and go down with them. Cipolla’s term is super-stupid.

Regional’s file photo is a man who saw a spider in his garage and reached for the lighter fluid. The garage is gone. The spider was unharmed.

Now you. Plot something you did this week. Tap the graph or work the sliders, and Regional will tell you what you are.

Start small. The thing in the group chat will do.

The Fourth Law

“It’ll be fine” will not be fine

The law, in plain EnglishPeople who aren’t stupid always underestimate how much damage a stupid person can do, and keep forgetting that dealing with one costs you. Every time. No exceptions.

The first three laws are about him. This one is about you. You know better, and you keep doing it anyway.

Regional has laid out five options. Pick whichever one looks safest.

It happens because you are reasonable. You assume he has a reason, because you would have one. You assume he will stop when it starts going badly, because you would stop.

He has no reason and he will not stop. You are waiting for a motive the way people wait at a bus stop on a discontinued route.

So you lend him the truck. You add him to the group chat. You let him run the grill, plan the bachelor party, hold the ladder. He is family, or he asked nicely, or you were tired.

Each one felt like the decent thing to do. Cipolla’s point is that the decent thing has a price here, every single time, and that you will have forgotten the price by Thursday.

Handwritten in the margin: DO NOT lend Dale the truck (again)

You do not even have to say yes. Regional notes that the number on the sheet just dropped while you were reading this.

You were standing near him. That counts.

Being right will not save you. A man in a lanyard tells you something is against policy. You ask to see the policy. There is no policy.

Now he is angry, the line is out the door, and you have won an argument and lost an afternoon. The same goes for correcting him online. You will be right by the third reply and still typing at midnight.

You were correct, you were polite, and everything is worse. That is the Fourth Law working exactly as designed.

The Fifth Law

Fear the idiot. The thief at least has a plan.

The law, in plain EnglishA stupid person is the most dangerous kind of person there is. More dangerous than a thief.

Regional ran the same quarter three times on paper. Same store, same menu, same number on the wall to start. The only thing that changed was who showed up.

First, a crew made entirely of thieves. Every shift somebody skimmed the tip jar. He was up two, the jar was down two, and the total never moved.

Thirteen weeks later the place was exactly where it started. Everyone hated each other and the Figure was a flat line. Regional has worked at worse.

You can manage a thief. He wants something, so you always know where he will be. He will be next to the something.

We are good at this. We have locks, cameras, passwords, insurance and an entire court system, and they mostly work, because a thief is predictable. He also needs the store to still be there tomorrow.

Handwritten in the margin: at least the thief brings donuts

Second, an honest crew with one Dale, unsupervised. Cipolla’s point is that a stupid person hits you without a motive or a plan, at the moment and in the place nobody thought to guard. You cannot stake out a man who does not know what he is about to do.

And every loss is a loss for everybody. No pocket fills up. Regional regrets that the chart ends at zero. The line does not.

Look around and you can see which one we planned for. Your front door has a lock for the burglar. It has nothing for the man deep-frying a frozen turkey on the back porch.

Your bank can stop a stranger from draining your account. It cannot stop your brother-in-law from investing it. There is no alarm that goes off when somebody says “watch this.”

Third, the same crew, the same Dale, and a few adults in the room. This is where Cipolla ends, and he was talking about whole countries.

A country on the way up and a country on the way down have the same share of stupid people. The difference is everybody else. Where the sensible ones stay on the job, the damage gets soaked up and the total still climbs. Where the people in charge turn into thieves who break more than they take, and everyone else gives up, he says it in five words: “the country goes to Hell.”
