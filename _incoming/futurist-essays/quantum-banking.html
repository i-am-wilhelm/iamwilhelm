QuantumBanking

The bill stalled. The bank got built anyway.

The CLARITY Act fell eleven votes short in the Senate in September. Inside the same eight weeks, regulators and the largest banks switched on the machinery the bill was written to govern. This is a map of that machine, a name for what it grows into, and a game plan for living next to it.

01 · The scoreboard

One statute stuck. Five pieces live.

Start with the facts, because the headline most people carry is wrong. The CLARITY Act passed the House 294 to 134 in July 2025 and cleared the Senate Banking Committee 15 to 9 in May 2026. On 15 September the Senate voted 49 to 50 on the motion to take it up, with 60 needed.12 Democrats then won both chambers on 3 November and take their seats on 3 January. The bill dies with this Congress unless the lame-duck session revives it, and prediction markets priced that in the low single digits.34

Read it top to bottom. The row that needed sixty senators is stuck. Every row that needed an agency or a bank consortium is moving.56789

One precision point on tokenized stock. The company still issues the share, and the SEC exemption covers trading only: no new shares may be sold on these venues.6 Banks and custodians mint the token that stands for a share sitting at the depository, and they carry it on their rails. That is the larger story. Whoever holds the token ledger holds the record of who owns what, minute by minute.

02 · Money mechanics

Three dollars

The same dollar now ships in three builds, and each one multiplies differently.

Build 1
Payment stablecoinBacked one for one, mostly by short Treasuries. Pays the holder no interest by statute. Every coin in circulation is a small loan to the government. This is narrow money: it moves fast and creates no credit on its own.
Build 2
Tokenized depositAn ordinary bank deposit recorded on a shared ledger. The bank still lends against it, deposit insurance is proposed to apply the same way, and it settles at 3 a.m. on a Sunday.9
Build 3
Tokenized collateralA share, a Treasury or a fund unit as a token. It can be pledged, released and pledged again in minutes where the paper version took days. DTCC plans a dedicated collateral chain early next year.7

The Chicago Fed's old booklet, Modern Money Mechanics, taught the deposit multiplier: a bank keeps a fraction, lends the rest, and the loan lands as a deposit somewhere else. The reserve requirement has been zero since March 2020, so that ratio stopped binding years ago.10 Credit today is limited by capital rules, liquidity rules and collateral. Tokenization leaves the capital rules alone and takes the friction out of collateral. The same asset can back a loan at nine, margin at noon and an overnight repo.

The reuse is already enormous on the slow rails. At 30 September, JPMorgan held $2.10 trillion of collateral it was permitted to re-pledge and had re-pledged $1.71 trillion of it, about 81 percent.11 Now make every hop in that chain instant and always open.

Run the chain

One $100 share token gets pledged. The lender advances the value less a haircut, then re-pledges what it received. Set the haircut and the number of hops.

Arithmetic only. Real chains are capped by re-pledging rules and by how much each counterparty will accept. The point is the shape: speed adds hops, and hops add credit.

That shape repeats at every scale, which is why fractal is the right word. A household borrows against a tokenized brokerage account. The broker pledges that position to a bank. The bank pledges its book to the central bank. Same pattern, larger each time, and after tokenization all of it clears on one clock.

Meanwhile the money layer is speeding up without growing. Stablecoin supply sat flat near $305 billion through the third quarter while quarterly transfers reached $891 billion, four times the level of early 2023, by one industry count.12 Citi projects that bank deposit tokens could carry $100 to $140 trillion a year by 2030.9 The pool stays the same size. The water moves faster.

03 · The name

Why call it quantum

The word here is a working metaphor. No quantum computer is involved. Three properties earn the name.

  1. QuantizedValue moves in the smallest unit anyone cares to define. A thousandth of a share. A tenth of a cent. One second of interest.
  2. SuperposedOne asset holds several jobs at once: savings, collateral, settlement balance. A settlement event fixes its state, and then it is free again.
  3. EntangledThe money and the record of who moved it travel as one object. Touch one and the other updates, wherever it is stored.

Fractional banking multiplied deposits. Quantum banking multiplies states. The third property is the one that reaches into your kitchen.

04 · The twin

Every token writes a row

The base layer of this system is identity-bound by law. Stablecoin issuers are financial institutions under the Bank Secrecy Act: they file suspicious-activity reports and screen every transfer against sanctions lists.13 They must be able to freeze a token on lawful order. Brokers report your proceeds, and from this year your cost basis.8 A cash purchase left no entry. A token purchase leaves a row with a name on it.

Now look at the clause the Senate fought over longest. The compromise text barred any reward that works like interest on a bank deposit, and allowed rewards tied to activity on a platform. Banks pressed to tighten it further.1415 Follow the incentive. A perk for holding is off the table. A perk for doing stays legal. Doing is data.

From here down: projectionNo rule on the books creates a registry or ties benefits to data. What follows is the incentive gradient, gamed forward.

The prototype already runs at the grocery store and the gas pump. The shelf shows two prices, and the lower one costs a phone number. Our parents clipped coupons. We scan a membership. The next version attaches to the money itself, and it scales to rent, insurance, utilities, transit and benefits.

Suppose a basic-income or expanded welfare program arrives on these rails. It lands as a programmable balance with the reward layer already attached. Small registries stack up quietly: verify your identity for lower fees, share your meter for a utility credit, link your pharmacy for a premium discount. For a household on one or two incomes, taking every discount becomes the only arithmetic that works. Collecting turns into a pastime with streaks, tiers and quests. Compliance arrives as a game people enjoy playing.

Your digital twin is the sum of those rows. Each one has a stored value, because each one prices a risk or predicts a purchase. The twin is a balance sheet you fill and someone else reads.

05 · The field

Three ways to live next to it

Collectors

The large majority. One or two wage incomes, possibly a transfer payment, and every discount switched on because the budget requires it.

Income
Wages plus programmable benefits
Privacy
Traded away in small, pleasant increments
Status
Tier level, streak length, points balance

The veiled

People who value privacy enough to pay for it. They keep a curtain here and there: a cash habit, a second wallet, a full-price shelf tag. On the money itself the veil is thin, because the rails know who they are.

Income
Usually more than one stream
Privacy
Bought at list price, month after month
Status
What they can afford to keep unrecorded

Operators

The builders and managers of the rails: people who run a node, a ledger, a compliant payment service, a clean set of entities. Their edge is small and real. Selling data is unnecessary for them. A tight ship is the whole business.

Income
Several streams, each through its own entity
Privacy
Structural: fewer events to report, each one documented
Status
Books that reconcile to the cent, on demand

The old status was the visible purchase. The new status is the clean ledger: few taxable events, every one accounted for, nothing an auditor could find that you had failed to find first.

06 · The game tree

Three branches from here

A

Long shot

Lame-duck revival

The motion to reconsider gets floor time before 3 January, or the bill rides on a must-pass funding or defense measure. As of this week nothing signals it.1

B

My base case for 2027 to 2028

The rewrite

A Democratic Congress drafts its own market-structure bill with heavier investor protection and ethics terms. The fight over who supervises and how rewards work starts again.4

C

Already happening

Rule by agency

No statute. The SEC exemption runs five years, the banking regulators finish the stablecoin rules, and private consortia set the standards. Law arrives later to ratify what exists.5

The rails get built on every branch. What varies is the supervisor, the reward rule, and how much consumer protection is bolted on. Branch C is running now and branch B is the likeliest thing to land on top of it. That is my read, and reasonable people weigh it differently.

07 · The plays

Six moves for a tight ship

  1. Make the books the assetTrack basis on every digital holding from 1 January 2026 forward, and reconcile it yourself. Assets moved between platforms arrive with no broker-reported basis, so your own record is the only one.8
  2. Count your taxable eventsSelling, swapping one token for another, and spending an appreciated token are each a reportable disposition. Holding is none. Fewer events means fewer rows and fewer bills.
  3. Know how the wealthy use Build 3Borrowing against an asset raises cash without a sale. Tokenized collateral brings that tool down-market. It also brings liquidation risk at machine speed, so size it for a bad week.
  4. Own a small toll boothThe durable income sits in running a piece of infrastructure well: settlement for a niche, compliant payments for a community, bookkeeping that speaks token. Margins are thin and steady.
  5. Price your data on purposeTake a discount when you have decided the row is worth the money. Keep household spending and business flows in separate identities and separate wallets.
  6. Watch one clauseWhatever bill comes next, read the stablecoin rewards section first. The line between rewarding holding and rewarding activity decides how hard the twin gets fed.

08 · The other reading

What the optimists see

Supporters of this buildout describe the same machine in brighter terms, and their case has weight. Instant settlement cuts cost for small businesses and for families sending money abroad. Full reserves make stablecoins safer than the uninsured balances many people already hold. Tokenized stock under the SEC's terms keeps shareholder rights intact and lets a company refuse. Reporting rules close gaps that mostly served evasion.

Critics on the other flank argue the rules are too loose: thin investor protection, conflict-of-interest gaps, and a risk that deposits leave community banks.15 A rewritten bill could add consumer-data limits that blunt the whole projection in section four.

Three things would prove this piece wrong: a rewards rule that bans activity-based perks outright, a privacy statute with teeth, or stablecoin use stalling as supply already has. Watch for them.
