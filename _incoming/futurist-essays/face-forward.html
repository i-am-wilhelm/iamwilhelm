Section 1 of 7

Stop Praying for the EMP

Fear keeps preppers in a box. Then they stock the box and call it freedom.

The EMP is the prepper's rapture. One flash and the world finally matches the pantry. Every bucket of rice becomes a throne.

The rapture has sponsors now. A guy with a ring light counts down to it every night, discount code in the description. Fear is the product. The bucket is the upsell.

Look at what that prayer asks for. A world sent back to the dirt, where the hard part is over and the only skills that count are the ones you already have. That's a fantasy of relevance wearing camo.

Here's what the fantasy skips. Your power going down is a local event. Whoever's sniffing around your door kept theirs on. The drone over your tree line has a full battery, and your trail cam has been phoning home since the day you plugged it in.

The ramshackle homestead needs cyberdefense. So does the Cold War bunker. Keep the dirt skills: grow the food, fix the pump, know your neighbors by name. Then add a firewall and you have a real plan.

Preppers and cyberpreppers are two cultures with two relationships to fear. One hides from the present inside a future disaster. The other stays in the present and deals with it.

Section 2 of 7

You Already Lost. Start There.

Cyberprepping is almost a funny word. Prepping assumes the bad thing is still on its way. On the cyber side it showed up decades ago, unpacked, and started charging rent.

Run the tape on the last hundred years and a pattern jumps out. The upgrade arrives with a meter attached.

Medicine

In 1910 one report, bankrolled by Carnegie money, graded every medical school in the country. Within a generation about half were gone, and the herbal and homeopathic schools took the hardest hit.

Cooling

Ammonia chilled the world cheap, and it was mean when it leaked. 1930 brought Freon: patented, branded, sold as the safe choice. Half a century later we found the hole in the sky.

Power

Tesla pitched energy sent through the air. The money walked. The meter stayed.

Media

Radio talked at you. TV sold to you. The phone listens back.

The black books

Some inventions go into a vault with a federal stamp on the door and come back out years later, if they come out at all. The Invention Secrecy Act has run that vault since 1951, and 6,471 secrecy orders were standing at the end of fiscal 2024 (Patent Office figures). When Tesla died in 1943, the government had his papers in custody within days.

Here's the pattern I'll stump on all day. The public gets the technology after the infrastructure is laid and the competitors are cleared. By the time it reaches the shelf, the toll booth is already built.

Each trade bought something real. Each one also moved a piece of your life behind somebody's counter: a fee, a patent, a subscription, a file with your name on it.

So privacy as a factory setting is dead. Bury it. What's left is privacy as a craft, something you build on purpose, like a fence or a root cellar.

Starting from the bottom is honest ground. You already know the score, and that frees you up to play.

Section 3 of 7

The Negotiation

The world turns regardless. It turned while you read that sentence.

Most lost time goes into a negotiation with yourself. Should I touch this thing. Is it cheating. Is it safe. What will it do to my soul. Round and round, year after year, and the meeting never adjourns.

Conscience belongs at the workbench. Bring it along and let it steer what you build and who it serves. Conscience performed from the sidelines is a hobby, and an expensive one, because the clock bills you either way.

Take the full inventory. What tools are on the table. What they can do today, in your hands. What's true about the world you wake up in. Then get to work.

One line item on that inventory: the thing probing your accounts at 3 a.m. never sleeps and never gets bored. The only guard with that stamina is another one of it, working for you. AI defends against AI. Plan accordingly.

Your ceiling is your own imagination. That's a strange and generous deal. Take it.

Section 5 of 7

At the Source

Pull your attention back from the black holes and it has to land somewhere. Spend it on one question: what CAN I do?

The list runs longer than fear says. Near the top sits one word: learn.

There has never been a better time to learn, and to learn properly. Off social media. The way we were taught before the internet. At the source.

Read the book, the whole one. Read the patent, the court filing, the study, the manual. Find the person who does the thing and watch their hands. Then put your own hands on it.

A feed hands you somebody's reaction to a summary of a rumor. The source hands you the thing itself. Minds get built on the second one.

The new tools belong inside that same discipline. Have the machine pull the original, walk it with you line by line, and then check its work. That's a library card with a tutor attached.

It is a wild fucking time to be a human. The horrors are real and so is the reach. Tell it in stories people understand, and go learn something at the source before lunch.

Section 6 of 7

Less Fear, More Magic

The futurist mind runs on a different fuel. It sees how fast the world turns without it and gets curious.

Curiosity is the whole trick. Fear asks what could go wrong and sits down. Curiosity asks the same question and keeps walking.

I have explosive ADHD brain matter. Ideas arrive forty at a time, and most of them used to die in the hallway. Now there's a tool that catches them, sorts them, and hands them back as something I can ship. Nothing with this kind of output existed for a mind like mine before.

That's the magic. More time in flow. New avenues for hope and action in the world right in front of me.

Every one of us is a warehouse of knowledge and experience, with stories worth telling. Most of those stories go to the grave untold because the telling took too long. That excuse just expired.

So build something beautiful. Build something useful. Better yet, make them the same thing.

Section 7 of 7

Grace, With the Lights On

Getting your mind right starts with getting real about what's around you. How we got here is a map. Study it once, fold it up, and face forward.

Face forward. Find grace. Know grace. Be grace.

Grace for the prepper, whose fear came from somewhere real. Grace for the skeptic still stuck in the negotiation. Grace for yourself, for every year you spent waiting on permission.

And keep the lights on while you do it. Grace opens the door. Discernment checks who's walking through it.

Gullible comes in two flavors. One believes every promise in the brochure. The other believes every prophecy of doom. Both handed their thinking to somebody else.

Both systems have value, the dirt and the data. One of them is the world you wake up in every day.

Build in the one that's real.
